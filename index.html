Robotic Gripper

At Northwestern's McCormick School School of Engineering I designed and fabricated a specialized robotic gripper integrated with custom sensors. This was for a key-in-lock research project to teach advanced robotic machine learning models to open a lock. The gripper acts as a hand would in the process allowing the robot to pick and place a key in a lock. Over three weeks this summer, I designed and built a two-finger parallel gripper drawing on UMI's hand-held gripper and the compact AgileX PiPER gripper as references. I first tried a geared design similar to BaRiFlex's rigid-linkage approach, but then moved to a direct linear-rail actuation system instead. From there I iterated through several frame and finger revisions, 3D-printing and rebuilding each time the structure flexed or misaligned under motion. Getting the actuation, rail, and finger geometry to move together as one rigid body was a challenge, and by the end of the three weeks I had a fully built gripper ready for sensing.

RC Rocker-Bogie Rover

In a design group we engineered a rocker-bogie suspension mars rover named 'Murder She Rover' for our design and fabrication course. I led the design and implementation of the rover's claw mechanism and its electronics. During PDR, I organized our slideshow and worked on the CAD for the arm joint, while teammates helped prepare the claw slides and others focused on the body and wheels. In the shop, I finalized and prototyped the claw extensively before shifting to help assemble the body and then working on the electronics integration. I also kept our BOM and materials organized throughout. The result was a rocker-bogie suspension system that traverses rough terrain with minimal body vibration, paired with a 4-DOF robotic arm and custom gripper, R/C-controlled through Arduino and dual 45A RoboClaw motor controllers.

Bike Wheel Transmission

I co-designed and fabricated a custom belt-drive transmission housed in a laser cut acrylic gearbox. This was done for a design competition testing maximum power transfer and efficiency with a $200 budget of which our team placed 4th overall. I led the mathematical analysis and organizational side of the design. During the PDR and CDR stages, I took the lead on organizing our slideshow and worked through the math behind the pulley system we ultimately chose, presenting that analysis to the team. I also worked extensively on the BOM, making sure we complied with the competition requirements and calculated the machining tolerances needed for each component. Once we moved into machining, I was comfortable enough in the shop to contribute there as well. Throughout the project, our team communicated well and each played to our strengths, my focus stayed on making sure the math checked out and the project stayed organized from concept through fabrication, resulting in a competitive transmission that we successfully demoed.
